docs: what the tool produced, stated so someone outside it can judge

Nine training rounds and 1046 boxes exist as console logs and as the numbers in
CLAUDE.md, which is written for whoever continues the work rather than for
someone deciding whether to believe it. docs/OVERVIEW.md is the second audience:
the result, what it cost, and what to distrust.

Every figure in it was read off disk rather than recalled — results.csv per run,
the label files for the class distribution, datasets/val for the validation
instance counts. The best mAP50-95 is 0.858 at round 8, not the 0.856 written
down at the time.

It says plainly that round 9 scoring lower than round 8 is not a regression: the
validation set is regenerated on every export, so the two are measured on
different frames. The one comparison that survives is per class on a bigger test
set, and palet_3lu improved there. A summary that quietly showed 0.858 as the
current number would be the easier document and the wrong one.

The weaknesses get their own section rather than a footnote, because they are what
an engineer will ask about: the 59:1 imbalance that explains every score below
0.75; that deduplication is why a rare class stays rare; that the audit cannot see
a consistent mistake, since the model was trained on the labels it is judging; and
that four of six classes are measured on three to eighteen instances, so their
third decimal place means nothing.

Also included are the two findings that outlast this dataset — that a class
getting worse as it gains data is dirty rather than starved, and that one wrong
label damages two classes — because they cost several rounds to learn and are the
part worth carrying into the next project.

CLAUDE.md's own state section was three rounds stale: 834 frames and round 6.
Updated, with the class counts in it, since those explain most of the per-class
scores and were the thing being recomputed by hand every session.
